<p style="margin-top:20px;"><em class='author-highlight'>I am on the job market for tenure-track faculty positions beginning in 2027.</em><br><br>
I am a fifth-year Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at Cornell University, where I am advised by <a class="page-link" href="https://mimno.infosci.cornell.edu">David Mimno</a> as part of the <a href="https://c2l.infosci.cornell.edu">C2L Lab</a>. I will graduate in May 2027. My research is in <span class='field-highlight'>applied artificial intelligence (AI)</span> and <span class='field-highlight'>natural language processing (NLP)</span>, motivated by problems from <span class='field-highlight'>computational humanities</span>. I contribute to both technical and literary domains: I use challenging questions about narratives to help define the boundaries of what large language models (LLMs) can do and to create datasets and methods for scholars in the humanities and social sciences. I am particularly interested in using domain expertise to inform computational approaches to scaling narrative research. I employ methods ranging from statistical modeling and human annotation to linear probes and cutting-edge applications of LLMs. My work has been published in core NLP venues (EMNLP, COLM) and domain-specific conferences and workshops (e.g., the Conference on Computational Humanities Research [CHR]). I am also committed to teaching students in CS and data science, with a particular interest in introductory classes.<br><br>
In the summer of 2024 I worked as a research assistant for <a href="https://www.au.dk/en/rdkm@cc.au.dk">Ross Deans Kristensen-McLachlan</a> at the <a href="https://chc.au.dk">Center for Humanities Computing</a> at Aarhus University. During the summer of 2025, I was a research intern in the <a href="https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/group/ai-interaction-and-learning/">AIIL Group</a> at Microsoft, supervised by <a href="https://www.kirantomlinson.com">Kiran Tomlinson.</a> Finally, in the summer of 2026, I was the instructor of record for INFO 1100: Introduction to Programming, which I re-designed and wrote the materials for from scratch.<br><br>
In 2022, I graduated from Carleton College with a Bachelors of Arts in Computer Science and English and a minor in Digital Arts and Humanities. I was advised by <a class="page-link" href="https://cs.carleton.edu/faculty/ealexander/">Eric Alexander</a> and <a class="page-link" href="https://www.carleton.edu/directory/gshuffel/">George Shuffelton</a>. I also participated in the <a class="page-link" href="https://eecs.berkeley.edu/resources/undergrads/research/superb">SUPERB REU</a> at UC Berkeley in the summer of 2021, where I was advised by <a class="page-link" href="https://schasins.com">Sarah Chasins</a> and <a class="page-link" href="https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~adityagp/">Aditya Parameswaran</a>.<br><br>
Please feel free to reach out to me <a href="mailto:rmh327@cornell.edu">by email</a> if you have any questions!</p><br>
